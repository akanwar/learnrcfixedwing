Chapter 06

Build the Structure

The aerodynamic design only exists in the air if the finished model is straight, stiff enough, light enough, and reliably connected.

Match the method to the mission

Balsa / plywood

  • Light, stiff, precise, repairable
  • Needs grain awareness, jigs, finishing

Foamboard

  • Fast, cheap prototypes; folded skins are strong
  • Paper + hot glue add sneaky mass

EPP / EPO molded foam

  • Tough, practical trainers
  • Adhesive compatibility matters; reinforce mounts

3D print / composite

  • Complex, repeatable shapes
  • Shells can be heavy or brittle; composites need process control (a future deep-dive volume)
Prototype the hardest joint first — wing joiner, gear plate, motor mount. A weak joint erases a strong material.

What a wing actually is

Cut the wing anywhere and the cross-section tells the story: spar caps (top and bottom) carry the bending as tension and compression; the shear web joins them so they work as one beam; the D-box skin from spar to leading edge resists twist; the rib holds it all in the airfoil shape.

Mroot ≈ n W b / 8   (uniformly loaded straight wing — a first-order check, not a structural analysis)
Your turnYour 1.5 kg trainer pulls a 60° bank (n = 2). Estimate the root bending moment — and would a bare 6 mm balsa spar take it?
M ≈ 2 × 14.7 N × 1.47 m / 8 ≈ 5.4 N·m — like holding a 1-litre bottle half a metre from your wrist, forever. A bare 6 mm balsa stick will not; a built-up spar with caps and a shear web will, at a fraction of the weight of “just use harder wood”.

Glue is structure

AdhesiveGood useWatch out
Thin CATight balsa jointsBrittle; fumes; bonds skin instantly
Medium CASmall gaps, field repairsFoam-safe type where required
Aliphatic / PVAWood-to-woodNeeds clamping and cure time
EpoxyMotor mounts, joiners, mixed materialsHeavy; mix accurately
Hot glueFoamboard prototypesHeavy; softens with heat

Design the joint so loads flow through overlapping material, doublers, and gussets. Glue transfers load; it should never bridge a bad fit by itself. More glue is usually heavier, not stronger.

Build straight — a repeatable sequence

  1. Prepare: full-size plan, protected flat board, matched ribs, labeled left/right.
  2. Pin the datum: lower spar or skin dead straight; root and tip blocks set incidence and washout.
  3. Skeleton: ribs square to the board; spars, webs, LE, TE, servo mounts.
  4. Lock in torsion: D-box or top skin while the panel is still jigged.
  5. Join panels: set dihedral with a gauge; verify both incidences match.
  6. Fit controls: bevel, hinge, horns, full travel without binding.
  7. Cover and re-check: shrink opposite sides in stages; measure twist again while warm.
You cannot “build the warp out later.” Covering can correct small errors, not a crooked structure. “Looks straight” is not a measurement — use one datum, strings, and matching diagonals (chapter 7 checklists).

Put the weight where the balance needs it

Fix the immovable masses first (motor, gear, servos), then create a battery adjustment range so the CG is tuned, not corrected with lead. Nose moments balance tail moments: Σ(m × distance) = 0 about the target CG. A heavy tail is the most expensive weight on the aircraft; long pushrods often beat tail-mounted servos.
