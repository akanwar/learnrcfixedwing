The Flight Plan

How this guide fits together, what to have on the bench, and the one safety rule that is never optional.

The one idea that organizes everything

A model airplane is a connected system. Change the weight and you change the wing area it needs; change the wing and you change the structure, the power, the balance, and often the tail. Good designers work around a loop, one deliberate change at a time.

Freeze the mission first. Otherwise the design grows heavier, faster, and more complicated every time a new feature wanders in.

Bench kit

Measuring & alignment

  • Steel rule and tape; digital scale (grams)
  • Incidence gauge or a phone inclinometer app
  • Flat building board, pins, clamps, string for diagonals

Electric & radio

  • Wattmeter (non-negotiable), LiPo charger + fire-safe bag
  • 4+ channel radio, receiver, 4 servos, ESC with BEC
  • Velcro, foam, zip ties, servo-lead retainers

Cutting & shaping

  • Fresh #11 blades, straightedge, sanding blocks in several grits
  • Covering iron or foam-safe glues, depending on build style

Field

  • An experienced pilot for the maiden — genuinely the best tool on this list
  • Big grass field, calm morning, charged everything, and a written plan

Safety, stated once and meant everywhere

Remove the propeller whenever you set up the radio, reverse channels, adjust endpoints, or work near the power system. An armed ESC plus a twitch of a transmitter stick is how builders lose fingertips.

Also: follow your local field rules and the AMA safety code, respect LiPo charging discipline, and never fly an aircraft that needs large unexplained trim.

Accuracy note This guide was drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by a human, but it will contain mistakes. Treat every number as a starting point, not gospel: verify against the linked references and your own measurements before you cut material or fly. Spotted an inaccuracy?
