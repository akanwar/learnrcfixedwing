Size the Wing

Start from mass and desired low-speed behaviour — span is a result, not a starting point. Two planes with the same span can fly nothing alike.

Wing loading: the number that predicts personality

wing loading = mass ÷ area   →   S = m ÷ target loading

In model units: area in dm² = mass in grams ÷ target g/dm². Lower loading means slower flight and gentler landings; higher loading means speed and wind penetration.

≈ 25–40 g/dm²≈ 40–60 g/dm²60+ g/dm²
Slow / floatyTrainer / sportFast / heavy — lands hot

Small models need proportionally lighter loading to feel as forgiving — they fly at lower Reynolds numbers (math shelf). Sanity-check against proven models of similar size.

Keep a mass budget with a 10–15% reserve for what you forgot. Weight added late forces faster flight, stronger structure, and a bigger power system — the loop bites.

The sizing chain

  1. Estimate all-up flying mass — battery, covering, fasteners, glue, everything.
  2. Choose a target wing loading for the mission.
  3. Area: S = m ÷ loading.
  4. Choose aspect ratio; span b = √(AR×S), chord c = S ÷ b.
  5. Check stall speed. Repeat until the numbers agree.
Vs = √( 2W / ρ S CLmax )

More weight raises stall speed by √2 per doubling, not 2. More area lowers it — and adds structure and drag. CLmax is the least certain input: treat the result as an estimate and verify at altitude. A calm-air approach near 1.3 Vs is a sensible habit.

Your turnA 2000 g version of the trainer keeps 45 g/dm². Find S and the span at AR 6.5.
S = 2000/45 = 44.4 dm² = 0.444 m² → b = √(6.5 × 0.444) ≈ 1.70 m. Weight grew 33% but span only 16% — area scales with b².

Aspect ratio and planform

AR = b² / S

Higher AR

  • Lower induced drag, efficient glide
  • Longer spar, bigger bending problem

Lower AR

  • Compact, agile, easy to make stiff
  • More induced drag

Rectangular / mild taper

  • Easy to build, friendly stall
  • The right answer for a first design

Strong taper / sweep

  • Tip-stall risk without washout
  • Sweep moves the aerodynamic centre — extra CG care

The four built-in angles

AngleWhat it isWhy you care
DihedralTips sit higher than the root (front view)A sideslip lifts the low wing — the plane rights itself
WashoutTip twisted 1–2° nose-down vs the rootRoot stalls first; ailerons keep working; stalls become a nod, not a snap
IncidenceWing chord angle vs the fuselage datumA construction setting fixed in the jig — not the same as angle of attack
DecalageWing incidence minus tail incidenceSets the elevator trim needed for level flight
Both wing panels must match, measured from the same datum with the same jig. Accidental twist is not washout, and covering can pull a straight wing crooked.
Your turnLeft tip measures +1.4° washout, right tip +0.6°. Washout, warp, or both — and which wing drops in a stall?
Both: intended washout plus 0.8° of warp. The right tip flies at a higher effective angle, stalls first, and the plane drops the right wing — usually on final, when you least want it.
