The Checklists

Evidence, not enthusiasm, moves a build forward. These are the four lists that keep it honest — print them and tick with a pencil.

Four lists, one habit: nothing advances because the calendar says so — it advances when the evidence says the stage is complete. Print them (the navigation hides itself) and tick with a pencil.

Build-stage gates

Design freeze — before cutting

  • Mission written downOne primary mission with numbers — target mass, stall speed, field size. Not adjectives.
  • Wing and tail sizedArea, span, chord and both tail volumes on the worksheet, sanity-checked against a proven model.
  • CG range and datum definedWhere the CG must sit, what you measure from, and how the battery moves to trim it.
  • Power chosen from dataMotor, prop, ESC and battery as a tested combination — not mixed from guesses.

Structure — before building

  • Load paths identifiedYou can point at the drawing and say how wing lift reaches the fuselage and where landing loads go.
  • Jigs and datums preparedFlat board, incidence blocks, matched rib sets — before any glue is opened.
  • Access designed inBattery, servos, receiver and cooling planned now, not carved out later.
  • Materials compatibility checkedAdhesives tried on scrap of the actual wood or foam; hardware sized to its loads.

Before covering

  • Incidence and washout measuredBoth panels read the same numbers from the same datum — written down, not eyeballed.
  • Tail square, fin verticalDiagonals match left and right; the fin checked from front AND rear.
  • Linkages cycle freelyFull travel, no slop, no binding, no servo buzz at the extremes.
  • Mass and CG updatedEstimates replaced with weighed numbers — and the balance plan still works.

Ready to maiden

  • Power verifiedFull-throttle amps, watts and temperatures measured with a wattmeter, cowling installed.
  • Battery, failsafe, range testPack restrained for crash loads; failsafe closes the throttle; range test per the manual.
  • Controls confirmedCorrect directions viewed from behind, low rates selected, trims centered.
  • Maiden plan agreedWritten flight plan and abort criteria, helper briefed — before driving to the field.
  • Legal to fly (US)FAA registration number on aircraft · TRUST certificate with you · Remote ID broadcasting or FRIA site — chapter 7.

Covering & alignment

Before covering

  • Wing panels match in incidence and washout.
  • Horizontal tail square to the fuselage centerline.
  • Fin is vertical.
  • Control surfaces straight; hinge gaps consistent.

During covering

  • Shrink opposite sides in stages.
  • Support the panel on a flat jig.
  • Recheck twist while the material is still warm.
  • Keep heat away from foam and plastic parts.

After covering

  • Measure root and tip angles again.
  • Check the wing join and fasteners.
  • Verify all controls move freely.
  • Open cooling paths and servo hatches.

Finishing discipline

  • Paint and filler have real mass.
  • Keep the tail especially light.
  • Balance the spinner and propeller.
  • Mark the CG points permanently under the wing.

Preflight — same order every time

1 · Airframe

  • Wing and tail secure.
  • No cracks, loose covering, or warps.
  • Propeller undamaged and correctly fitted.
  • Landing gear and wheels secure.

2 · Controls

  • Correct direction viewed from behind.
  • Full movement; no binding or buzz.
  • Neutral positions match marks.
  • Hinges, horns, clevises and retainers secure.

3 · Power

  • Correct battery and charge state.
  • Pack restrained; hatch latched.
  • Cooling path clear.
  • Throttle cut works; prop area clear.

4 · Radio / range

  • Correct model memory.
  • Low rates selected.
  • Failsafe verified.
  • Range test as the radio manufacturer specifies.
Anything that fails gets fixed before flying — not watched during it.

The maiden-flight plan

  • 1 · ConditionsLarge familiar field, mild steady wind, good light, charged batteries, experienced helper. US: FAA registration number on aircraft · TRUST certificate with you · Remote ID broadcasting or FRIA site.
  • 2 · Ground checkCG, directions, range, power, launch plan, timer, emergency throttle cut.
  • 3 · TakeoffFirm smooth launch or straight ground roll; no extreme climb angle.
  • 4 · Climb to heightReach safe altitude before trimming, slowing, or aggressive turns.
  • 5 · Trim lightlyOne axis at a time. Large trim needed → land and inspect instead of masking it.
  • 6 · Basic checksGentle turns, power-off glide, control response. No low-altitude stall tests.
  • 7 · Land earlyWide pattern, extra speed. Save advanced testing for later flights.
Abort if the airplane is badly out of trim, controls feel wrong, power is weak, or the structure sounds abnormal. An abort is a successful test.
